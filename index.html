Research

My work brings together robotic perception, autonomous flight, and inspection in challenging indoor environments.

01 Autonomous inspection

Autonomous UAV inspection in GPS-denied environments

I work on autonomous aerial inspection in indoor environments, including dark spaces where GPS is unavailable. This research connects localization, navigation, and inspection data acquisition to support the assessment of built environments.

A key direction is linking visual observations to their spatial locations, so inspection findings can be represented on a floor plan.

Indoor navigation / Spatial representation / Visual inspection
02 Visual localization

Learned visual features for UAV localization

This work investigates the use of learned visual features, including XFeat and SuperPoint, with VINS for UAV localization. It includes comparisons on public datasets and experiments with a physical UAV.

Visual-inertial estimation / Feature detection / Flight experiments
03 Robotic systems

From sensing to autonomous flight

My experience includes hardware and sensor integration for a LiDAR-equipped UAV, alongside the software needed for localization and autonomous operation. Working with physical systems shapes my interest in methods that can be deployed and evaluated beyond simulation.

LiDAR / Sensor integration / Real-world robotics

About

Connecting a civil engineering background with research in mobile robot autonomy.

I hold a master's degree in Civil Engineering, with a focus on computer-aided engineering. My research experience centers on developing autonomous aerial robotic systems for challenging indoor environments.

I am interested in perception, planning, and control for physical mobile robots, and in how learning can help robots operate reliably in real-world settings.
